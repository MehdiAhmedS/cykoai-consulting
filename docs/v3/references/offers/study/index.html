About this study

Each offer opens as it reaches you, with a small 3D piece of the work.

Rows open on their own as they scroll up past the lower third of the screen, the way Cuberto's do, and close again if you scroll back. The copy is the current draft from the site. The 3D pieces share the globe's look: Windsor solids with a light ramp, white hairline edges, and Driftwood for the one thing that moves the story on.

Security reviews

Three layers of an app (interface, API, data) with a scan passing through. Where it passes, points on a layer flag and ring.

Workspace hardening

Accounts around the organisation, inside a faceted shell. Freelancer links are Driftwood, and one at a time their access is pulled back.

AI automation

Irregular tasks arrive on three lanes, pass through one gate, and leave as an even, single stream.

Mobile and devices

A phone with its app, sending commands to a sensor, a hub and a tag, and getting data back along arcs like the globe's.

APIs and bots

A bot inside its container (the deployment), turning to call each service around it: a database, messaging, the web, payments, files and events. Each call goes out in Driftwood and the reply comes back in white.

Company profiles

A page that separates into its layers: the grid, the layout, the type and the brand, then settles back into one.

One renderer

All six pieces share a single WebGL renderer, drawn into each row's own canvas. Only rows that are open and on screen draw.

Same three.js

The site already loads three.js for the globe, so these reuse that code rather than adding a second engine.

Still when needed

With reduced motion, every row is open and each piece shows one still pose. Phones could get stills too, like the globe.
